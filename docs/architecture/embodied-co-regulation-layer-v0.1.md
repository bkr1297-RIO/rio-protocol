## One-line definition

The **Embodied Co-Regulation Layer** is the physical-world extension of ONE/RIO/MUSS: governed machines, robots, prosthetics, exosuits, wearables, home systems, and community devices that may support human life without becoming authority over humans.

---

## Core pattern

> Machine presence without machine authority.

A physical MUS Unit may be present in the world, assist people, support routines, route help, and preserve dignity. It may not become a cop, parent, judge, therapist, employer, public authority, or autonomous enforcer.

---

## Public-safe compression

> RIO Guardian Bots are weaponless MUS Units designed to support, de-escalate, route help, and preserve human dignity in physical environments.

## Body-world stack

| Layer | Function |
|------|----------|
| Human / Community Authority | Defines purpose, limits, consent, local norms, escalation rules, complaint paths |
| MUSS | Holds sovereignty, identity boundary, scope, revocation, consent, proof requirements |
| RIO | Governs admissible consequence before physical action |
| Sentinel | Prevents execution drift, coercive behavior, unsafe mismatch, or action outside packet |
| MUS | Produces receipts for consequential interactions, refusals, holds, and escalations |
| Ledger / Chronicle | Preserves proof and human-readable history when implemented |
| MANTIS / Convergence | Watches drift, trust patterns, complaint patterns, overreach, rubber-stamping |
| Local Humans | Maintain, supervise, repair, tune, govern, audit, and override |

The robot, exosuit, or device is not the system. The system is the governed relationship among machine body, human authority, local rules, safety constraints, proof, and review.

## Embodied Assistance Clause v0.1

A physical MUS Unit may assist, orient, de-escalate, notify, guide, record permitted events, and summon authorized human help.

It may not punish, command, detain, coerce, intimidate, profile, or silently escalate consequence beyond its charter.

Plain version:

> Help, don't rule. Calm, don't control. Route, don't replace.

---

## Exosuit Sovereignty Clause v0.1

A governed exosuit may amplify, stabilize, protect, assist, or translate human movement only within the wearer's explicit control, safety constraints, and revocation rights.

It may not override the wearer's agency except under narrowly defined emergency-stop conditions that are visible, reviewable, and receipted.

Plain version:

> The suit may strengthen the human. It may not replace the human's will.

## Household / Family Co-Regulator boundary

A home MUS Unit may support:

- bedtime routines;
- elder care reminders;
- child calming routines;
- family schedule coordination;
- conflict de-escalation prompts;
- medication reminders;
- emergency detection;
- safe check-ins;
- chore scaffolding;
- emotional transition support.

It may not become the parent, judge, therapist, disciplinarian, or household authority.

Public-safe line:

> A household MUS Unit helps the home remember, regulate, and route support without taking over the family's authority.

## Community integration requirement

Physical MUS Units require local human integration.

Minimum ecosystem roles:

| Role | Function |
|------|----------|
| Maintenance teams | Keep physical systems safe and functional |
| Behavior tuning teams | Adapt tone and responses to local culture under policy |
| Cybersecurity teams | Prevent hijack, spoofing, drift, and tampering |
| Community liaisons | Gather feedback, maintain trust, explain boundaries |
| Local governance boards | Set rules, limits, complaint channels, review conditions |
| Receipt auditors | Review logs, blocked actions, escalations, and failures |
| Safety reviewers | Evaluate physical movement, proximity, force, and liability |
| Human responders | Receive routed requests and make human decisions |

Keeper line:

> The bot is only safe if the community can correct it.

## Failure modes

| Failure mode | Description | Control |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| Authority mimicry | Robot posture, voice, lights, or placement make it feel like police/security | Design language and local review |
| Escalation drift | Bot calls authorities or emergency services too quickly | Local escalation policy + audit |
| Profiling | Distress, homelessness, anger, disability, or confusion becomes risk identity | Pattern Dignity rules |
| Privacy breach | Public interactions become surveillance | Data minimization + visible recording rules |
| Community rejection | People experience the robot as tech-dropped control | Co-design and complaint channels |
| Hijack/tampering | Robot is hacked, spoofed, or remotely redirected | Signed commands, cybersecurity, fail-safe shutdown |
| Physical harm | Movement, navigation, proximity, or force injures someone | Certification, geofencing, speed/force limits |
| Liability fog | Nobody knows who is responsible | Clear owner/operator/maintainer/auditor roles |
| Exosuit override | Suit moves, locks, or resists against wearer will | Wearer authority and emergency-stop rules |
| Labor coercion | Employers use augmentation to demand unsafe productivity | Worker-rights governance and policy limits |
| Medical overclaim | Device promises therapeutic benefit without evidence | Regulatory discipline and claim review |
| Dependency lock-in | Human autonomy is reduced by vendor/system dependency | Portability, revocation, open standards where possible |
