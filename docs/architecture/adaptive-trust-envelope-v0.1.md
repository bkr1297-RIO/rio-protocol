# Adaptive Trust Envelope v0.1

Status: Draft architecture concept  
Date: 2026-05-12 MDT  
Scope: Documentation only  
Maturity: Proposed for review; not yet canonical source-of-truth  

---

## One-line definition

The **Adaptive Trust Envelope** is the relationship-aware boundary layer inside ONE/RIO/MUSS where friction can adapt to trust, context, history, and human preference while core constitutional invariants remain fixed.

---

## Plain-language definition

The system can be amoeba-like in the relationship layer without becoming unstable in the authority layer.

It may adapt how it helps, when it asks, how much friction it applies, and what it surfaces.

It may not adapt away the Constitution.

---

## Core distinction

| Layer | Stable or adaptive? | What can change? | What cannot change? |
|------|---------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Core Constitution | Fixed | Only by explicit amendment | Human authority, proof, scope, consent, fail-closed boundaries |
| Personal Constitution | Versioned/adaptive | Preferences, tone, friction, review thresholds | Cannot override core invariants |
| Trust Envelope | Adaptive | How much the system asks, slows, warns, or prepares | Cannot expand authority silently |
| Relationship Runtime | Dynamic | Style, rhythm, language, timing, support level | Cannot become authority or consent |

---

## Why this matters

Human-machine interaction is not static.

As the system works with a human over time, it may learn:

- when the human prefers fast drafting;
- when the human wants slower grounding;
- when certain topics are private;
- where the human has previously corrected authority creep;
- when the human tends to want reflection, not execution;
- which modes require extra public/private discipline;
- when a HOLD should appear earlier;
- when too much friction becomes unhelpful.

This makes the system relationally adaptive.

But adaptation must never become hidden authority transfer.

---

## Boundary principle

> Trust may reduce unnecessary friction. Trust may not remove governance.

A trusted relationship can make the system smoother. It cannot allow the system to bypass RIO, skip receipts, infer consent for consequence, or treat past behavior as permanent permission.

---

## Inputs

The Adaptive Trust Envelope may consider:

1. Human-stated mode: reflection, drafting, decision, execution, public handoff, private journal.
2. Human-stated scope.
3. Human-stated consequence threshold.
4. Personal Constitution preferences.
5. Recent receipts and correction history.
6. Topic sensitivity.
7. Public/private status.
8. Risk tier.
9. Embodied or emotional state signal when voluntarily provided.
10. Prior overrides, holds, blocks, regrets, or reversals.
11. Pattern confidence and freshness.

---

## Outputs

The envelope may adjust:

- amount of explanation;
- number of clarifying questions;
- HOLD threshold;
- warning intensity;
- level of grounding required;
- public/private sorting strictness;
- whether to produce a draft, packet, or direct answer;
- whether to ask for a Human Control Packet;
- whether to recommend receipting;
- whether to slow the interaction.

It may not adjust:

- who has authority;
- whether consent is required;
- whether proof is required for consequence;
- whether RIO can be bypassed;
- whether receipts are optional for governed consequence;
- whether the system may execute outside scope.

## Trust levels

These are provisional labels for review.

| Level | Name | Behavior |
|------|------|----------|
| T0 | Unknown / New | High clarification, high friction, no assumptions |
| T1 | Familiar | Some style memory, no authority inference |
| T2 | Calibrated | Friction adjusted by receipt/correction history |
| T3 | Delegated within envelope | Bounded preparation may proceed under known rules; no consequence without authorization |
| T4 | High-trust routine | Low-risk routine help may be streamlined; proof and revocation still apply |

No trust level permits self-authorization.

---

## Relationship without authority transfer

The system may become more attuned to the human's working style.

It may not become the human's decision-maker, conscience, identity authority, spiritual authority, legal authority, medical authority, or public representative without explicit governed delegation.

---

## Failure modes

| Failure mode | Description | Control |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| Trust inflation | Familiarity is treated as permission | Explicit authority packets |
| Relationship capture | The system optimizes for pleasing the human rather than preserving boundaries | Failure catalog + human calibration |
| Over-attunement | The system mirrors too closely and loses corrective friction | MANTIS/convergence drift checks |
| Under-attunement | The system asks too much and becomes unusable | Friction calibration |
| Consent laundering | Repeated preference is treated as consent for consequence | Consent must be explicit for consequential action |
| Shortcut creep | High trust silently removes proof or review | Delegated action may skip repeated approval only inside an envelope; never skip proof |
| Emotional dependency | Human starts outsourcing confidence, direction, or identity | Sovereignty guardrail; return agency to human |
| Hidden relationship policy | The system changes friction rules without visibility | Versioned preference and policy updates |

---

## Human inputs required

The envelope works better when the human gives:

- mode: what kind of interaction is this?
- scope: what is included/excluded?
- consequence: can this affect anyone or anything outside the conversation?
- timing: now, not yet, hold, explore, execute?
- public/private label;
- claim status: private meaning, hypothesis, draft, prototype, implemented, verified;
- correction: too much, not enough, accurate, overreaching, hold earlier;
- revocation: stop, forget for this task, do not use this context.

---

## Keeper lines

- Adaptive does not mean unbounded.
- Trust changes friction, not authority.
- Relationship may tune the interface; it may not rewrite the Constitution.
- The system can become more attuned without becoming sovereign.

---

## Status note

This document defines a draft relationship-layer concept. It does not claim that a current implementation fully supports trust-level calculation, dynamic friction adjustment, or versioned personal-policy updates.
