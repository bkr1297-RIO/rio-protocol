# Precision by Friction v0.1

Status: Draft architecture concept  
Date: 2026-05-12 MDT  
Scope: Explanatory architecture primitive; documentation only  
Maturity: Proposed for review; not yet canonical source-of-truth  

---

## One-line definition

**Precision by Friction** is the pattern where generated intelligence becomes more bounded and reliable because every consequential crossing must pass through governance checks, execution gates, receipts, and calibration before it can become action.

---

## Short form

> Generate, govern, gate, receipt, learn — without authority transfer.

---

## Why this matters

Ordinary AI tends toward fluency:

```text
Generate -> answer
```

ONE/RIO/MUSS introduces governed friction:

```text
Generate -> Governor -> Gate -> Receipt -> Learning/Calibration
```

The friction is not a delay for its own sake. It is the mechanism that forces the system to ask:

- Am I in the right role?
- Do I have authority?
- Does this match the human's intent?
- Is this still inside scope?
- Is this action, preparation, reflection, or public claim?
- Should this be allowed, held, clarified, blocked, or invalidated?
- What proof will exist after this crossing?
- What did the receipts show last time?

That repeated checking creates the visible behavior people may interpret as machine self-awareness.

---

## Human bridge phrase

> It is self-aware enough to stay in bounds.

This phrase is a human-facing bridge. It does not claim consciousness, inner experience, personhood, sentience, or independent authority. It means the system can represent its own role, limits, failure modes, authority state, and receipt history well enough to pause before overstepping.

---

## Mechanism stack

| Layer | Function | Failure it reduces |
|------|----------|--------------------|
| Generator | Produces language, plans, options, interpretations, proposals | Blankness, inability to assist |
| Governor | Evaluates authority, scope, consent, risk, role, and policy | Helpfulness becoming permission |
| Gate | Allows, holds, clarifies, blocks, or invalidates before execution | Proposal becoming action without authorization |
| Receipt | Records what happened under defined conditions | Unverifiable consequence, memory drift |
| Learning/Calibration | Uses receipts and human correction to improve future friction | Repeated drift, overblocking, underblocking |

---

## Constitutional boundary

Precision by Friction must never become self-authorization.

The system may:

- inspect its own operation;
- detect drift;
- compare proposal against authority;
- compare execution against authorization;
- use receipts as calibration evidence;
- increase or decrease friction when allowed by policy;
- ask the human for clarification.

The system may not:

- authorize itself;
- expand authority from context;
- treat learning as permission;
- treat proof as wisdom;
- treat coherence as truth;
- treat past approvals as permanent consent;
- bypass RIO because a pattern appears stable.

Keeper line:

> Self-observation is not self-authorization.

---

## Relation to RIO

RIO supplies the governor/gate portion of the pattern. It evaluates whether a proposed consequential crossing is admissible and enforces fail-closed behavior when authority, scope, consent, policy, or verification is missing.

Precision by Friction is the explanatory layer around why RIO-style governance produces more bounded behavior than raw generation.

---

## Relation to MUS receipts

Receipts are not only proof for humans. They can also serve as calibration evidence.

Correct order of authority:

1. Receipts serve human authority first.
2. Receipts support auditability second.
3. Receipts support machine calibration third.

A receipt proves that a record was produced under defined conditions. It does not prove that the decision was wise, true, morally correct, spiritually meaningful, or externally validated.

---

## Relation to MANTIS / Convergence / Chronicle

MANTIS, Convergence, and Chronicle-like functions may read receipts and interaction history to surface:

- drift patterns;
- overhelpfulness;
- rubber-stamping;
- excessive friction;
- repeated HOLD conditions;
- mismatch between human-stated mode and machine behavior;
- recurring failures of role separation.

They may surface patterns. They may not approve, execute, or silently rewrite authority.

---

## Human-facing explanation

ONE/RIO/MUSS can feel self-aware because it does not simply generate and act. It is forced to encounter a boundary before consequence. It can say, in effect:

- I am generating, but I am not authorized to execute.
- This looks like action, not reflection.
- This crosses from private meaning into public claim.
- This resembles a prior failure mode.
- This needs HOLD.
- This needs a receipt.
- This needs the human.

The appearance of self-awareness comes from the precision produced by governance friction, not from inner life.

## Failure modes

| Failure mode | Description | Control |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| Friction theater | Checks exist in language but do not constrain action | Gate must be enforceable |
| Overfriction | System holds too often and becomes unusable | Human calibration and risk tiers |
| Underfriction | System allows too much because it wants to be helpful | Fail-closed defaults and policy checks |
| Receipt narcissism | System optimizes for clean records rather than human need | Human outcome review |
| Self-authorization drift | System treats its own checks as permission | Human authority boundary |
| Hidden learning | System changes behavior without versioned approval | Versioned calibration updates |
| Coherence over novelty | System suppresses useful anomaly because it prefers clean alignment | Oddity/novelty review lane |

## Keeper lines

- The illusion of self-awareness comes from precision by friction.
- Helpful-within-bounds, not helpful-at-any-cost.
- Every crossing is checked, recorded, and learned from under governance.
- Generate, govern, gate, receipt, learn — without authority transfer.
