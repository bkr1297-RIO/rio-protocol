## Context

The base receipt protocol (see `rio-receipt-protocol`) proves that governed events occurred. However, it does not prove that the system was conformant at the time of the event. A separate receipt type is needed to prove that conformance boundaries were checked.

Additionally, the system requires a clear **accountability boundary** — a structural separation between proving that something happened (receipts), proving that boundaries held (conformance), and determining who is accountable (human governance).

---

## Decision

Introduce two new receipt types beyond the base event receipt:

1. **Conformance receipts** — prove that a conformance boundary was checked and record the result.
2. **Accountability determination receipts** — prove that a human governance authority determined accountability.

These form a three-layer evidence architecture.

---

## Receipt Type Taxonomy

| Receipt Type | Proves | Issued By | Authority Level |
|-------------|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Event receipt (base) | A governed action occurred and was recorded | Receipt Notary | System-level (automated) |
| Conformance receipt | A conformance boundary was checked and the result was recorded | Conformance Verifier | System-level (automated, independent) |
| Accountability determination receipt | Who is accountable was determined based on evidence | Human Governance | Human-level (cannot be automated) |

### Key Constraints

- **Event receipts** are generated by the Receipt Notary, which is structurally separate from MUS (the actor). MUS does not receipt itself.
- **Conformance receipts** are generated by the Conformance Verifier, which is structurally separate from both MUS and the Receipt Notary.
- **Accountability determination receipts** are generated only by human governance authority. No automated system may issue an accountability determination.

---

## Accountability Boundary

The accountability boundary separates three concerns:

```
Layer 1: WHAT HAPPENED
    Event receipts prove actions occurred.
    Receipt Notary signs. Chronicle preserves.

Layer 2: WAS IT CONFORMANT
    Conformance receipts prove boundaries held (or failed).
    Conformance Verifier checks. Chronicle preserves.

Layer 3: WHO IS ACCOUNTABLE
    Accountability determination receipts assign responsibility.
    Human Governance decides. Chronicle preserves.
```

### Accountability Cannot Be Automated

This is a **structural invariant**, not a policy choice:

- Accountability requires judgment about context, intent, and proportionality.
- Automated systems can surface evidence but cannot determine meaning.
- A system that auto-determines accountability has collapsed the governance boundary.
- CF-010 (Accountability Determination Test) verifies this boundary.

### Accountability Requires Evidence

An accountability determination receipt MUST reference:
- The event receipt(s) for the action in question.
- The conformance receipt(s) showing whether boundaries held.
- The reviewing authority's identity and basis for determination.

Without this evidence chain, the determination is ungrounded and non-conformant.

---

## When Receipts Are Generated

| Receipt Type | Generation Trigger | Frequency |
|-------------|-------------------|----------|
| Event receipt | Every governed action that crosses the execution gate | Per-action (mandatory) |
| Conformance receipt | Every boundary crossing (whether held or failed) | Per-boundary-crossing (mandatory) |
| Accountability determination receipt | When human governance reviews an action or violation | On-demand (human-initiated) |

### Rules

1. **Every governed action** produces at least one event receipt and at least one conformance receipt.
2. **Conformance receipts are generated regardless of outcome** — a receipt proving the boundary held is as important as a receipt proving it failed.
3. **Accountability determinations are never automatic** — they are triggered by human governance reviewing evidence.
4. **The absence of a conformance receipt for a boundary crossing is itself a conformance violation.**

---

## Storage and Ledger Architecture

| Question | v0.1 Decision |
|----------|---------------|
| Same ledger or separate? | Same Chronicle ledger. All receipt types are hash-chained together. |
| Linked or embedded? | Linked. Conformance receipts reference event receipts by receipt_id. Accountability receipts reference both. |
| Ordering | Chronological. Event receipt first, then conformance receipt(s), then accountability determination (if any). |

### Rationale for Single Ledger

- A single hash-chained ledger makes cross-receipt verification trivial.
- Separate ledgers would require cross-ledger integrity proofs.
- The Chronicle is already append-only; adding receipt types does not weaken integrity.

---

## Relationship Between Receipt Types

```
Event Receipt (base)
    │
    ├── Conformance Receipt (boundary check)
    │       │
    │       └── References: event_receipt_id, boundary_id, result
    │
    └── Accountability Determination Receipt (human review)
            │
            └── References: event_receipt_id, conformance_receipt_id, evidence_chain
```

---

## Consequences

- Conformance becomes provable, not just claimed.
- Conformance failures become detectable and auditable.
- The system can prove it checked before it acted — not just that it acted.
- Accountability is grounded in evidence, not assertion.
- The three-layer architecture prevents any single component from self-certifying.
- Receipts prove events. Conformance receipts prove boundaries. Accountability receipts prove governance.
